# Balcony and Urban Gardening Guide

Small balconies, patios, and sunny windowsills can support a useful garden
when containers are chosen carefully. Start by observing how many hours of
direct sunlight reach each spot. Fruiting plants such as tomatoes and peppers
usually need at least six hours, while leafy greens and mint tolerate partial
shade. Check that the balcony can safely support filled pots, especially
after rain makes soil heavier.

Choose containers with drainage holes and place saucers underneath to protect
the floor. A lightweight potting mix is more reliable than dense garden soil
because it drains well and gives roots room to grow. Group plants with similar
water needs. Basil, parsley, and chives grow well together; rosemary prefers
a drier pot and should not share a container with thirsty greens.

Water slowly until a little drains from the bottom. During hot weather, check
containers each morning, since small pots dry faster than garden beds. Mulch
the surface with a thin layer of straw or shredded leaves to reduce moisture
loss. Feed vegetables with a balanced liquid fertilizer according to its
label rather than adding extra in the hope of faster growth.

Inspect leaves weekly for aphids, mildew, and yellowing. Remove damaged leaves
and improve airflow before using any treatment. Rotate pots so plants receive
even light, and support climbing beans or tomatoes with secure stakes. Keep a
simple notebook of planting dates, watering, harvests, and weather. Over time,
those notes reveal which varieties suit the space and which containers need
adjustment.
